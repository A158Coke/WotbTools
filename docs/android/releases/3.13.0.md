# Android 3.13.0

- versionName: 3.13.0
- versionCode: 3013000
- Native Bridge: v3, unchanged
- Version classification: minor — the bundled armor viewer now uses Armor Inspector's per-layer penetration probability with BlitzKit-aligned per-layer normalization/2× rules, deterministic 1× heatmap consumption, measured camera calibration, theme-following scene styling, and canvas-anchored result cards.
- Minimum supported version: unchanged at 3000000; optional viewer feature and fixes do not require a forced update.

The armor viewer's probability layer uses the Armor Inspector Gaussian interval (Blitz ±5% treated as ±3σ), evaluates every non-external layer, subtracts one effective thickness per layer, and treats external modules as flat consumables; continuation segments initialize once at their segment start; both the click probability chain and the GPU/JS continuation stop at the first Primary (far-side hull/track hits are excluded). The deterministic judgment and primary heatmap follow BlitzKit `shoot()` layer-by-layer normalization and 2×-caliber enhanced normalization, use one effective-thickness deduction per layer, and share the geometric face normal between the click raycast and the GPU heatmap. The heatmap keeps the red→orange→yellow→green probability ramp so its color semantics match the result card. The single-tank armor/shot camera uses the measured 15 m / 18.75° reference with the 2.8-product far cap; the two-tank world overview keeps native 50°.

Known heatmap limitation: probability is exact for zero or one spaced layer, including an external module entirely in front of it (RT1 moduleInFront shifts the spaced CDF by the module flat); two or more spaced layers, or modules on both sides of the spaced stack, use one aggregate Gaussian CDF because RT0 is additive and loses the per-layer prefix/order. The click card remains the authoritative per-layer chain; exact GPU tracing is deliberately deferred for mobile cost (see docs/frontend/architecture.md, 2026-10-18 note).

This source candidate does not establish staging, device acceptance or production publication.
